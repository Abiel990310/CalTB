Part 4 · Contextual Applications of Differentiation

Motion, rates, and L'Hôpital

Derivatives as rates in context — where the units carry the meaning, and where an indeterminate limit finally becomes easy.

By the end of this chapter you can

  • Interpret a derivative in context, with units
  • Analyse rectilinear motion from a position function
  • Distinguish displacement from total distance
  • Apply L'Hôpital's rule and know when it does not apply

A derivative is a rate of change, and in context the units say what kind. That sounds like a triviality and is worth most of a chapter, because “interpret this derivative” is asked on every exam and is marked on the sentence, not the number.

Units carry the meaning

If V(t)V(t)V(t) is volume in litres and ttt is time in minutes, then V′(t)V'(t)V′(t) is in litres per minute — a rate of filling. The unit is not decoration; it is the interpretation.

An answer that says “V′(5)=−3V'(5) = -3V′(5)=−3” earns less than one saying “at 5 minutes the tank is draining at 3 litres per minute”. The second states the sign’s meaning and the units, which is what the rubric asks for.

Three questions the exam asks from exactly this setup:

When is it at rest? v=0v = 0v=0, so t=1t = 1t=1 and t=3t = 3t=3.

When does it change direction? When vvv changes sign — which it does at both, since each is a simple root. A double root would touch zero without crossing, and the object would not turn.

When is it speeding up? When vvv and aaa share a sign. Between t=1t=1t=1 and t=2t=2t=2, vvv is negative and aaa is negative, so it is speeding up while moving backwards — the case that catches people, and the same point PhysTB’s chapter 1.1 makes.

Displacement is not distance

displacement=∫abv dttotal distance=∫ab∣v∣ dt\text{displacement} = \int_a^b v\,dt \qquad \text{total distance} = \int_a^b |v|\,dtdisplacement=∫ab​vdttotal distance=∫ab​∣v∣dt

Displacement is net change in position and can be zero for a long journey. Total distance never decreases.

To compute total distance, split at every time the velocity changes sign and add the magnitudes. Integrating vvv straight through lets the backwards stretch cancel the forwards one — which is the right answer to a different question.

Pitfall

“How far did it travel?” means total distance. “How far is it from where it started?” means displacement. A question using the word total almost always wants the absolute value, and the split.

Undefined behaviour

Check the form first, every time.
