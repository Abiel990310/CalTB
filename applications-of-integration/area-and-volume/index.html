Part 8 · Applications of Integration

Area and volume

Integration accumulates. Choose the slice, write its contribution, and add them up — which is the same procedure for every problem in this unit.

By the end of this chapter you can

  • Find the area between two curves
  • Compute a volume by discs, washers, or known cross-sections
  • Choose the variable of integration deliberately
  • Recognise the one procedure underneath all of them

This unit looks like a list of formulas and is not. Every problem in it is the same three steps:

  1. Slice the region into pieces of thickness dxdxdx or dydydy.
  2. Write one slice’s contribution — its area or volume.
  3. Integrate across the range the slices cover.

Memorising four formulas is harder than learning that, and it fails on the fifth problem.

Area between curves

A slice is a thin rectangle of height “top minus bottom” and width dxdxdx:

A=∫ab(f(x)−g(x)) dxA = \int_a^b \big(f(x) - g(x)\big)\,dx A=∫ab​(f(x)−g(x))dx

Pitfall

“Top minus bottom” means top for that slice, not top overall. If the curves cross inside the interval, they swap, and a single integral computes the difference of signed areas rather than the total area.

Split the integral at every crossing, or integrate ∣f−g∣|f - g|∣f−g∣, which is the same thing written shorter. A negative area is the symptom that this was missed.

Undefined behaviour

Square the radii separately and then subtract. R2−r2R^2 - r^2R2−r2 is not (R−r)2(R - r)^2(R−r)2, and substituting the height of the region for the radius difference is the single most common error in this unit.

Choosing the variable

Slice perpendicular to the axis of rotation, and in the direction that avoids splitting the region.

  • Rotating about a horizontal axis → slices are vertical → integrate dxdxdx.
  • Rotating about a vertical axis → slices are horizontal → integrate dydydy, which means rewriting the curves as xxx in terms of yyy.

A region whose top boundary changes partway along needs two integrals in xxx but often only one in yyy. Spending thirty seconds choosing can halve the work, and the choice is where the marks quietly are.

Recap

  • Every problem here is: slice, write one slice’s contribution, integrate.
  • Area between curves: ∫(top−bottom)\int (\text{top} - \text{bottom})∫(top−bottom), split wherever they cross.
  • Discs: π∫f2\pi\int f^2π∫f2. Washers: π∫(R2−r2)\pi\int (R^2 - r^2)π∫(R2−r2), squaring before subtracting.
  • Known cross-sections change only the slice’s area formula; the procedure is identical.
  • Slice perpendicular to the axis of rotation, and choose the variable that avoids splitting the region.
