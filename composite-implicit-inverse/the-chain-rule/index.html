Part 3 · Differentiation: Composite, Implicit, and Inverse Functions

The chain rule

The rule for a function inside a function, and the one students most often apply without noticing they needed to.

By the end of this chapter you can

  • Identify the outer and inner function in a composition
  • Differentiate nested compositions correctly
  • Explain why the inner derivative appears as a factor
  • Recognise a composition disguised as something simpler

Everything so far differentiated functions that were added, multiplied or divided. The chain rule handles the remaining way to build a function: putting one inside another.

ddxf(g(x))=f′(g(x))⋅g′(x)\frac{d}{dx} f\big(g(x)\big) = f'\big(g(x)\big)\cdot g'(x) dxd​f(g(x))=f′(g(x))⋅g′(x)

In words: differentiate the outside, leave the inside alone, then multiply by the derivative of the inside.

That final factor is the whole rule. Forgetting it is the single most common mistake in differential calculus, and it is silent — the answer looks plausible.

Why the inner derivative is there

Think about rates. If yyy changes three times as fast as uuu, and uuu changes five times as fast as xxx, then yyy changes fifteen times as fast as xxx. Rates through a chain multiply:

dydx=dydu⋅dudx\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du}\cdot\frac{du}{dx} dxdy​=dudy​⋅dxdu​

which is the same statement in Leibniz notation, and is the version worth remembering because the cancellation is a useful mnemonic — even though the symbols are not really fractions being cancelled.

Look at the first: sin⁡(2x)\sin(2x)sin(2x) oscillates twice as fast as sin⁡x\sin xsinx, so its slope is twice as steep. The factor of 222 is not bookkeeping — it is the function genuinely changing twice as quickly.

Drag hhh towards zero at x=0x = 0x=0: the slope heads for 222, not 111. The derivative of sin⁡x\sin xsinx at zero is 111; compressing the input doubles it.

Nesting deeper

Three layers is the same rule applied twice, working outward in:

ddxf(g(h(x)))=f′(g(h(x)))⋅g′(h(x))⋅h′(x)\frac{d}{dx} f\big(g(h(x))\big) = f'\big(g(h(x))\big)\cdot g'\big(h(x)\big)\cdot h'(x) dxd​f(g(h(x)))=f′(g(h(x)))⋅g′(h(x))⋅h′(x)

Peel one layer at a time and write each factor as you go. The mechanical discipline matters more than cleverness here — most errors in nested problems are dropped factors, not misunderstood rules.

Pitfall

The most common failure is not getting the chain rule wrong; it is not noticing it applies.

ddxsin⁡(x2)≠cos⁡(x2)\frac{d}{dx}\sin(x^2) \ne \cos(x^2) dxd​sin(x2)=cos(x2)

That answer would be right if the inside were xxx. Because it is x2x^2x2, a factor of 2x2x2x is missing — and the wrong answer is perfectly well-formed, so nothing looks amiss.

Before differentiating anything, ask whether the argument is just xxx. If it is not, a chain rule factor is owed.

Where it shows up next

The chain rule is not one topic among several. It is the machinery behind:

  • implicit differentiation — every yyy is secretly y(x)y(x)y(x), so differentiating it produces dydx\tfrac{dy}{dx}dxdy​ as an inner derivative,
  • related rates — every quantity is secretly a function of ttt,
  • uuu-substitution in integration, which is the chain rule run backwards.

Getting comfortable now pays three more times before the course ends.
