Part 1 · Limits and Continuity

Computing limits

Four techniques, and a rule for choosing between them: substitute, factor, rationalise, or recognise a known limit.

By the end of this chapter you can

  • Evaluate a limit by direct substitution and say when that is legal
  • Resolve a 0/0 form by factoring or by rationalising
  • Recognise the standard limits that no algebra will produce
  • Choose the right technique from the shape of the expression

Chapter 1.1 said what a limit is. This one is about getting the number.

There are only four moves, and the shape of the expression tells you which to reach for. Start by substituting — not because it always works, but because what it produces tells you what to do next.

Move 1: substitute

If fff is built from polynomials, roots, exponentials, sines and cosines by the usual arithmetic, and the substitution produces an ordinary number, then that number is the limit.

lim⁡x→3(x2−4x+1)=9−12+1=−2\lim_{x \to 3} \left(x^2 - 4x + 1\right) = 9 - 12 + 1 = -2 x→3lim​(x2−4x+1)=9−12+1=−2

This is not laziness; it is a theorem. Those functions are continuous on their domains, and continuity is precisely the statement that the limit equals the value. Chapter 1.3 makes that formal.

Substitution can produce three kinds of result, and each is an instruction:

What you get What it means
An ordinary number That is the answer. Stop.
nonzero0\tfrac{\text{nonzero}}{0}0nonzero​ The limit does not exist; check the signs for ±∞\pm\infty±∞
00\tfrac{0}{0}00​ Indeterminate. Do more work — moves 2, 3 or 4

Pitfall

00\tfrac{0}{0}00​ is not an answer and not zero and not one. It is the algebra telling you it has no opinion, because some functions of that shape approach 222, others approach 000, and others have no limit at all. The form tells you nothing; only further work does.

Move 2: factor and cancel

When substitution gives 00\tfrac{0}{0}00​ in a rational function, the numerator and denominator share a factor of (x−a)(x - a)(x−a). Find it, cancel it, substitute again.

lim⁡x→3x2−9x−3=lim⁡x→3(x−3)(x+3)x−3=lim⁡x→3(x+3)=6\lim_{x \to 3}\frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3} = \lim_{x \to 3}\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x - 3} = \lim_{x \to 3}(x + 3) = 6x→3lim​x−3x2−9​=x→3lim​x−3(x−3)(x+3)​=x→3lim​(x+3)=6

The cancellation is legal for the reason chapter 1.1 gave: the two expressions agree everywhere except at x=ax = ax=a, and the limit never looks at x=ax = ax=a.

Why the shared factor must be there. If substituting aaa makes the numerator zero, then aaa is a root of it, and a polynomial with root aaa has (x−a)(x - a)(x−a) as a factor. Same for the denominator. So the 00\tfrac{0}{0}00​ form guarantees the cancellation exists — you are never searching blindly.

Move 3: rationalise

A root in the expression blocks factoring. Multiply by the conjugate instead, which converts a difference of roots into a difference of squares.

lim⁡x→01+x−1x=lim⁡x→01+x−1x⋅1+x+11+x+1=lim⁡x→0xx(1+x+1)=12\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{\sqrt{1+x} - 1}{x} = \lim_{x \to 0}\frac{\sqrt{1+x} - 1}{x}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{1+x} + 1}{\sqrt{1+x} + 1} = \lim_{x \to 0}\frac{x}{x\left(\sqrt{1+x} + 1\right)} = \frac{1}{2}x→0lim​x1+x​−1​=x→0lim​x1+x​−1​⋅1+x​+11+x​+1​=x→0lim​x(1+x​+1)x​=21​

The middle step is the whole trick: (1+x−1)(1+x+1)=(1+x)−1=x(\sqrt{1+x}-1)(\sqrt{1+x}+1) = (1+x) - 1 = x(1+x​−1)(1+x​+1)=(1+x)−1=x, and that xxx cancels the one downstairs. The conjugate does for roots exactly what factoring does for polynomials — it produces the shared factor that the 00\tfrac{0}{0}00​ form promised.

Move 4: recognise

Some limits yield to no algebra at all. These are worth memorising, because the exam expects them and no manipulation produces them:

lim⁡x→0sin⁡xx=1lim⁡x→01−cos⁡xx=0lim⁡x→0ex−1x=1\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x} = 1 \qquad \lim_{x \to 0}\frac{1 - \cos x}{x} = 0 \qquad \lim_{x \to 0}\frac{e^x - 1}{x} = 1x→0lim​xsinx​=1x→0lim​x1−cosx​=0x→0lim​xex−1​=1

Note the fourth: 1−cos⁡xx→0\tfrac{1-\cos x}{x} \to 0x1−cosx​→0 but 1−cos⁡xx2→12\tfrac{1-\cos x}{x^2} \to \tfrac{1}{2}x21−cosx​→21​. The numerator vanishes like x2x^2x2, not like xxx — so which denominator you divide by changes the answer entirely. Students who memorise only the first of those get the second wrong every time.

These three are the derivatives of sin⁡\sinsin, cos⁡\coscos and exe^xex at zero, which is why they turn up everywhere in chapter 2.

Composing them

Most real limits need two moves. sin⁡5xx\dfrac{\sin 5x}{x}xsin5x​ is not a standard limit, but it becomes one after a rearrangement:

sin⁡5xx=5⋅sin⁡5x5x\frac{\sin 5x}{x} = 5 \cdot \frac{\sin 5x}{5x} xsin5x​=5⋅5xsin5x​

and as x→0x \to 0x→0 the inner argument 5x→05x \to 05x→0 too, so the fraction tends to 111 and the whole thing to 555.

The pattern: make the argument and the denominator match, then the standard limit applies and the constant comes out front.
